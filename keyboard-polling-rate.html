Demystifying Keyboard Polling Rate, Scan Rates & Input Lag

In modern esports and competitive PC gaming, input latency has moved to the center of performance discussions. While gaming mice popularized high polling rates (1000Hz, 4000Hz, and 8000Hz), gaming keyboards have recently introduced hyper-polling architectures. However, understanding how keyboard reporting works is essential to interpreting test results.

Why Keyboards Don't Constantly Send Packets (Interrupt vs Bulk)

Unlike an optical gaming mouse that constantly captures surface delta changes (streaming up to 8,000 coordinate packets every second during motion), a keyboard operates on an event-driven state-change model:

  • When you are not touching any keys, the keyboard sends zero report frames or sends empty keep-alive packets at a baseline idle rate.
  • When a key is actuated, the internal microcontroller registers the closed contact, constructs an 8-byte HID report, and pushes it to the USB endpoint buffer.
  • Therefore, to test keyboard polling frequency, users must trigger rapid key changes across multiple fingers (sweeping or rapid alternating chord bursts).

Mechanical Debounce Delay vs Hall Effect Rapid Trigger

Traditional mechanical switches use two physical copper leaf contacts. When you press a key, the leaves bounce microscopically for 5 to 15 milliseconds before settling. Keyboard firmware must apply a "debounce algorithm" (such as defer or eager debounce) to ignore bouncing, introducing artificial delay. In contrast, Hall Effect magnetic switches measure magnetic flux analogously, eliminating mechanical contact bounce entirely and delivering near-instant 0.1ms actuation.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why is my keyboard showing 500Hz or 1000Hz instead of 8000Hz?

Many 8000Hz keyboards (like Razer Huntsman V3 or Corsair K70 MAX) default to 1000Hz in their companion software (Synapse or iCUE) for maximum CPU efficiency. Ensure 8000Hz is explicitly toggled in your keyboard software and connected to a native motherboard USB 3.0 port (not a hub).

How accurate are browser-based keyboard polling tests?

Modern Chromium and Gecko engines report event.timeStamp using microsecond-precision hardware clocks (based on performance.timeOrigin). While background Windows thread scheduling can add occasional 0.5ms jitter, burst sampling captures the true minimum packet delta.

Can 8000Hz keyboard polling cause game stuttering?

Unlike mouse 8000Hz which generates massive continuous hardware interrupts, keyboards generate vastly fewer events (rarely more than 50 to 100 key presses per second). Consequently, 8000Hz keyboards have negligible impact on CPU frame pacing.

What is N-Key Rollover (NKRO)?

NKRO means the keyboard can register every single key simultaneously without "ghosting" or dropping inputs. Standard office keyboards only support 6KRO (6 keys simultaneously).
