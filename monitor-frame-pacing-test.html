The Science of Frame Pacing: Why High FPS Can Still Feel Stuttery

Most PC gamers evaluate performance solely by glancing at an in-game FPS counter. However, Average FPS is a deeply flawed metric. If a game delivers 120 frames in the first 500 milliseconds and only 20 frames in the second 500 milliseconds, the FPS counter proudly displays "140 FPS"—yet the human eye perceives severe, nauseating hitching.

The "Golden Rule" for G-Sync / FreeSync Setup

To achieve arcade-cabinet smoothness on PC without introducing controller or mouse input latency, adhere to Blur Busters' verified VRR configuration:

  • Enable G-Sync / FreeSync: In your GPU control panel (NVIDIA Control Panel or AMD Software: Adrenalin Edition).
  • Enable V-Sync Globally in GPU Driver: Set Vertical Sync to "On" in the NVIDIA Control Panel (keep in-game V-Sync turned OFF). When G-Sync is active, GPU V-Sync prevents frame tearing without adding traditional double-buffer input delay.
  • Set Frame Rate Cap (-3 FPS Below Refresh): Cap your maximum framerate 3 FPS below your display refresh ceiling (e.g. 141 FPS on a 144Hz screen, 237 FPS on a 240Hz screen). This ensures your frame rate never bounces into the hard V-Sync ceiling, eliminating input lag completely.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why does this test detect my 144Hz monitor as 60Hz?

Many users purchase a 144Hz or 240Hz monitor but forget to change Windows display adapter settings from the factory default 60Hz. Open Windows Settings > System > Display > Advanced Display and set your refresh rate to its maximum rated frequency.

Can hardware acceleration in Google Chrome cause stutters?

Yes. Ensure "Hardware Acceleration" is enabled in browser settings. If disabled, software CPU rendering causes massive 50ms frame time spikes and dropped frames.

What is a 1% Low and 0.1% Low?

1% low represents the average framerate of the slowest 1% of frames during a session. If your average is 200 FPS but your 1% low is 45 FPS, you will experience sudden micro-freezes during intense combat scenes.

Does Windows 11 Game Mode improve frame pacing?

Yes. Windows 11 Game Mode automatically prioritizes GPU and CPU thread affinity for the active foreground window, reducing background Windows updates and virus scans from causing frame time drops.
