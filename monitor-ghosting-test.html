🔬 The Physics of Monitor Ghosting: GtG vs. MPRT

When evaluating motion clarity on a gaming monitor, two fundamentally different physical metrics govern what you see: GtG (Grey-to-Grey Response Time) and MPRT (Moving Picture Response Time).

Grey-to-Grey (GtG) measures the physical duration required for liquid crystal molecules inside the panel to physically rotate and change from one shade of grey to another. If a monitor refreshes at 144Hz (producing a new frame every 6.94 milliseconds), but the liquid crystals require 12ms to shift colors, the pixels cannot finish transitioning before the next frame is pushed. The leftover old pixel states create physical ghosting.

MPRT (Moving Picture Response Time), by contrast, is dictated by the sample-and-hold nature of modern LCD and OLED displays. Because a frame remains illuminated continuously for the full frame duration, your eye tracks moving objects smoothly across the screen while the frame is stationary, causing retinal motion blur. The formula is straightforward:

MPRT (ms) ≈ 1000 / Refresh Rate (Hz)

At 60Hz, MPRT blur is 16.6ms. At 144Hz, it drops to 6.9ms. At 240Hz, it reaches 4.1ms. Even an OLED with a near-instant 0.03ms GtG response time will still exhibit eye-tracking motion blur unless high refresh rates or backlight strobing (BFI) are engaged.

🛠️ 4 Actionable Steps to Reduce Monitor Ghosting

1. Calibrate Monitor OSD Overdrive

Reach behind your monitor and open the hardware OSD menu. Look for Response Time or Overdrive (TraceFree / AMA). Switch it to "Fast" or "Medium". Never use "Fastest / Extreme" unless you want glaring white corona overshoot.

2. Engage Motion Blur Reduction (BFI)

If your monitor supports backlight strobing (such as ASUS ELMB, BenQ DyAc, or ULMB), enable it. Strobing strobes the backlight between frames, cutting sample-and-hold MPRT blur down to under 1.0ms.

3. Verify Native Maximum Refresh Rate

Many users buy 144Hz or 240Hz monitors but leave Windows running at 60Hz. Open Windows Settings > Display > Advanced Display and ensure your monitor is set to its highest native refresh rate over DisplayPort 1.4 or HDMI 2.1.

4. Smartphone Pursuit Camera Method

To scientifically capture ghosting without human eye adaptation, pan your smartphone smoothly alongside the moving UFO at 960 px/sec. Take a snapshot with a shutter speed of approximately 1/4th of your refresh rate (e.g. 1/60s for 240Hz).

Frequently Asked Questions

Can ghosting permanently damage my monitor?

No. Ghosting is entirely an optical characteristic of liquid crystal switching speed and sample-and-hold persistence. It causes zero physical wear or damage to the panel itself.

Why does my VA panel have terrible purple/black trailing when scrolling?

This is known as VA black smearing. Vertical Alignment panels excel at deep black levels, but transitioning from 0% black to 10% dark grey can take up to 30ms on budget VA panels. Tuning Overdrive and raising the monitor's Black Equalizer or Gamma slightly will mitigate the effect.

Does G-Sync or FreeSync introduce ghosting?

Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) dynamically changes your monitor's Hz. If your monitor lacks "Variable Overdrive", an overdrive setting tuned for 144Hz may cause severe inverse overshoot when your framerate drops to 70FPS. High-end G-Sync modules use variable overdrive to prevent this.
