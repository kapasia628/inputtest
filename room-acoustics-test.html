The Physics of Room Acoustics & Reverberation Time (RT60)

When speaking into a microphone in an untreated bedroom or home office, less than 50% of the acoustic energy captured by the capsule is your direct voice. The remainder consists of sound waves that bounced off drywall, glass windows, hard desks, and bare floors—arriving milliseconds later as destructive reflections.

Sabine's Formula: How Acoustic Panels Absorb Reverb

In 1898, physicist Wallace Clement Sabine formulated the universal law of room reverberation:

RT60 = 0.161 * (Room Volume in m³) / (Total Surface Absorption 'A')

Hard drywall has an absorption coefficient of only 0.05 (reflecting 95% of sound), while high-density open-cell acoustic foam absorbs up to 85% of high-frequency speech reflections.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why does Discord make my room sound like a bathroom?

Discord uses Krisp AI noise suppression. While Krisp removes constant computer fan noise, erratic room reverb causes AI artifacts that make your voice sound metallic, robotic, and underwater. Fixing room acoustics at the source is the only clean remedy.

What is a flutter echo?

A flutter echo is a rapid, ringing ping sound produced when sound bounces back and forth repeatedly between two parallel bare walls. Clap your hands in an empty room: if you hear a high-pitched metallic ping, you have severe flutter echo.

Does a microphone pop filter remove room echo?

No. Pop filters and foam windscreens only stop direct bursts of breath (plosives like P and B sounds) from overloading the capsule. They do not alter room sound reflections.

Where should I place acoustic panels first?

Use the 'mirror trick': sit at your desk and have a friend slide a mirror along the side walls. Wherever you see your microphone in the reflection is a primary reflection point that needs an acoustic panel.
