Why Does a Mouse Double Click by Itself?

Understanding the mechanical failure behind switch bounce chatter — and why software fixes it permanently without buying new hardware.

1

Metal Spring Fatigue

Computer mice use mechanical microswitches (such as Omron D2FC or Kailh GM). Inside is a microscopic copper-alloy spring. After 1 to 2 million clicks, metal elasticity degrades.

2

Contact Bounce Chatter

Instead of connecting smoothly, the weakened contact physically "bounces" against the electrical terminal. This sends 2 or 3 electrical pulses within 5 to 30 milliseconds.

3

ClickShield Suppression

Human fingers cannot physically click faster than 70 milliseconds. ClickShield allows the first click instantly, then absorbs all bounce chatter arriving under your set threshold.

Does ClickShield add input lag in fast-paced FPS games? ↓

No, zero input lag. When you click, ClickShield forwards that primary click to Windows and your game immediately with 0.00ms latency. The software only monitors the millisecond window directly after the first click to trap unwanted secondary bounces.

What is the best debounce threshold setting? ↓

We recommend starting at 50 milliseconds. Most switch chatter occurs between 10ms and 35ms. If your mouse still occasionally double-clicks on rare occasions, you can adjust the slider up to 60ms or 70ms. Casual users who click slowly can safely set it up to 80ms.
